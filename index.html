UX Midterm

Tracking Campus Shuttle

An end-to-end UX research process — from empathy and observation to information architecture and prioritization — for one of the most common broken experiences on campus: not knowing when the shuttle will arrive.

Phase 1

Empathy & Definition

Before proposing anything, the goal here was to understand the user's world as it actually is — starting with assumptions, then replacing them with real observation and real conversations.

Step 1

Proto-Persona

Name

Aaryan

Background

Aaryan is a B.Tech student at Vijaybhoomi University. He takes the college shuttle bus every day.

Goals

  • Get to campus on time for lectures and exams.
  • Catch his train on time when going back home.

Assumed Current Behaviour

Aaryan thinks the bus usually comes between 8:00–8:15 AM and leaves by 8:20 AM. But this isn't fixed — the bus doesn't always follow this timing.

Frustrations

  • He has to stand at the bus stop for 15–20 minutes every day without knowing when the bus will actually show up.
  • Because the timing is unpredictable, he's often late for lectures and exams.
  • He also risks missing his train on the way back home.

Quote

"I never know exactly when the bus will arrive, so I end up either waiting or worrying about being late."

Step 2

Observation Log

Location: Campus bus stop Time: 8:00 AM – 8:30 AM

What Was Observed

  • The student reaches the stop at 8:00 AM.
  • He checks his phone again and again.
  • He keeps looking down the road to see if the bus is coming.
  • At 8:15 AM, he makes a phone call.
  • The bus arrives at 8:20 AM.
  • He boards the bus quickly.

Step 3

Interview Notes

Raw Responses From 3 Real Students

  1. "There is no fixed arrival time; the bus often arrives either early or late."
  2. "Sometimes the bus leaves campus late. One student missed their train and had to wait 30 mins for the next train."
  3. "There is limited space for seating and luggage on the bus, as it is shared by faculty members, admin staff, and students."
  4. "Sometimes the bus changes, which results in lack of seating due to difference in seating capacity between buses."
  5. "Most of the seats are occupied by staff; only a few seats are available for students."
  6. "The price is too high compared to local buses, since there is no difference in the quality or service between the two."
  7. "Students feel there should be separate buses for students and college staff."

Step 4

Real User Persona

Name

Aaryan

Role

B.Tech student at Vijaybhoomi University, commutes daily via the campus shuttle.

Goals

  • Reach campus on time for lectures and exams.
  • Catch the connecting train on time while returning home.

Observed Behaviour

  • Arrives at the bus stop early, waits without any confirmed timing.
  • Checks phone repeatedly while waiting.
  • Sometimes calls a friend to ask about the bus's location.

Facts Gathered

  • No fixed arrival/departure time for the shuttle.
  • A missed shuttle connection has caused at least one student to miss a train, resulting in a 30-minute wait.
  • The shuttle is shared with faculty and admin staff, resulting in limited seating.
  • Seating capacity differs between buses, sometimes forcing students to stand.
  • Shuttle cost is seen as high relative to local buses.
  • Students have expressed a preference for separate buses for students and staff.

Step 7

Core Pain Point

"Students have no reliable way to know the campus shuttle's real-time location or arrival time, forcing them to wait blindly at the bus stop or depend on informal methods like calling a friend, causing daily anxiety and repeated lateness to lectures, exams, and trains."

Step 8

User Stories

  • As a student waiting for the shuttle I want to see the bus's real-time location, so that I know exactly how long I need to wait instead of guessing.
  • As a student planning my morning I want an estimated arrival time for the shuttle, so that I can decide when to leave without wasting time standing at the stop.
  • As a student catching a connecting train I want to be notified if the shuttle is delayed or cancelled, so that I can adjust my plans in advance.

Phase 2

Ideate & Evaluate

With the pain point and user stories in hand, this phase turns understanding into structure — brainstorming features, testing how real students naturally group them, and building a menu structure validated by actual navigation behaviour.

Step 1

Feature List

Tracking & Info

  1. Live bus location and estimated time of arrival at any stop.
  2. Seat occupancy status (which seats are occupied vs. available).
  3. Nearest stop finder, so students can board from any nearby stop to save time.
  4. Daily bus schedule or timetable.

Notifications & Alerts

  1. Notifications when the bus is near the stop, or if there's a delay or cancellation.
  2. Departure reminder notification, sent shortly before the student needs to leave to catch the bus.

Payments

  1. In-app payment method, so there's no time wasted paying manually.
  2. Monthly pass purchase/renewal within the app.

Support

  1. Request to the driver or bus in-charge to wait a few extra minutes if a student is running late.
  2. Contact the bus driver or in-charge directly for emergencies.

Step 2

Card Sort — Patterns Found

Tested the 10 features with 10 real students, asking each of them to group the features into categories that made sense to them. Here's what was found:

10/10Payments always stood alone

All 10 people grouped "In-app payment" and "Monthly pass" together, separate from everything else. Students clearly see payment-related things as their own topic, unrelated to tracking or notifications.

10/10Driver Support always stood alone

All 10 people also grouped "Request driver to wait" and "Contact driver for emergency" together, separately from the other features — both are about directly reaching out to the driver, not checking information.

8/10Core tracking features clustered together

"Live bus location," "Seat occupancy," and "Nearest stop finder" were grouped together by most students. They think of these three as answering the same basic question — where is the bus, and can I get on it?

2/10 disagreedWhere does "Daily Schedule" belong?

8 out of 10 people put "Daily schedule" with the tracking features, but 2 grouped it with notifications instead — since a schedule can feel like "bus info" or like "a reminder for when to leave." The majority view was used for the final structure.

2/10 disagreedWhere does "Seat Occupancy" belong?

Most people grouped "Seat occupancy" with tracking, but 2 people grouped it with notifications, thinking of it more as "get told if a seat opens up" rather than "check bus info."

Overall Takeaway All 10 students naturally created about 4 groups on their own, without being told to. This showed the feature list already had a clear, natural structure — students think of the app in terms of "tracking the bus," "getting notified," "paying," and "talking to the driver." This structure was used to directly build the app's first menu (V1 IA).

Step 5

Failure Analysis

Minor mix-ups within Bus Tracking & Schedule

Two students confused "Seat Occupancy" and "Daily Schedule" with "Live Bus Location." All three feel like "checking bus info," so students sometimes clicked the first tracking-related option instead of the exact right one. This is a small mix-up — all three options were still in the correct category.

The important one: passive vs. active confusion

One student picked "Notifications & Alerts" instead of "Support & Communication" when asked how to ask the driver to wait. This revealed a real mental-model gap: getting told something by the app (like a delay alert) is passive — the app is informing you. Asking the driver to do something is active — the user is making a request. Both felt like "app notifications" to this student, so the difference between "the app talking to me" and "me talking to the driver" wasn't clear.

Minor overlap between two notification types

One student mixed up "Delay Alerts" with "Departure Reminder." Both are notifications, so this is a small labelling overlap, not a bigger structural problem.

What Changed — V1 → V2 To fix the main issue (the driver-request confusion), two labels were renamed to make their meaning clearer: "Request Driver to Wait" became "Ask Driver to Wait for Me" (making clear this is something the user actively does), and "Delay/Cancellation Alerts" became "Bus Delay/Cancellation Notice" (making clear this is information coming from the app, not an action).

Phase 3

Prioritization

With a validated feature set and structure in place, the final step was deciding what actually gets built first — under the constraint of only four Must-Have features.

Step 1

MoSCoW Categorization

Must-Have
  • Live bus location and estimated time of arrival at any stop.
  • Daily bus schedule or timetable.
  • Notifications when the bus is near the stop, or if there's a delay or cancellation.
  • Nearest stop finder, so students can board from any nearby stop to save time.
Should-Have
  • Request to the driver or bus in-charge to wait a few extra minutes if a student is running late.
  • Monthly pass purchase/renewal within the app.
Could-Have
  • Contact the bus driver or in-charge directly for emergencies.
  • In-app payment method, so there's no time wasted paying manually.
Won't-Have
  • Departure reminder notification, sent shortly before the student needs to leave to catch the bus.
  • Seat occupancy status (which seats are occupied vs. available).
